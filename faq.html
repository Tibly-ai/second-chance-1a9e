Frequently Asked

Everything you're wondering before you say yes.

Fostering a dog is a real decision, not a small favor, so we'd rather you go in with clear eyes. Below are the questions we hear most from folks who reach out to Second Chance Fosters for the first time. If we missed yours, our number's at the bottom of every page.

01 What does fostering actually cost me? +

Fostering costs you time and a little floor space, not your wallet. We cover food, a crate, a leash, and any medical care your foster dog needs while they're staying with you. You bring the couch room and the patience for house-training.

02 Who pays for vet visits and supplies? +

We do. Every foster dog arrives with a folder of medical records and a direct line to our vet team. If your foster needs a checkup, medication, or an emergency visit, call us first and we'll handle the scheduling and the bill. Food, bowls, and bedding are provided too, though donated blankets are always welcome.

03 How long does a foster commitment last? +

Most stays run two to six weeks, long enough for a dog to decompress and for us to learn their personality before matching them with an adopter. Puppies or dogs recovering from a minor procedure sometimes need just a week or two. Seniors, or dogs mid-way through heartworm treatment, may stay a few months. We'll always tell you the expected timeline before you commit to a specific dog.

04 What if it turns out fostering isn't a good fit? +

It happens, and that's fine. If a foster dog isn't settling into your home, or your household isn't set up quite the way you thought, call us. We'll come get the dog, no guilt trip attached. Fostering only works if it works for your family too.

05 Can I foster if I have kids or other pets? +

Many of our best foster homes have both. When you apply, tell us about your household: kids' ages, current pets, cat or dog experience. We match you with a foster dog whose temperament fits, walk you through a slow introduction with resident animals, and we're upfront about any dog who isn't a good match for young kids or cats.

06 How does the application process work? +

Fill out the short form on our Get Involved page. We'll call within a few days to talk about your home, your schedule, and what kind of dog would suit you. Next is a quick home visit or video walkthrough, and once you're approved, you're usually matched with your first foster within a week or two.

07 What happens once I say yes to a dog? +

We set a pickup time, hand off supplies and paperwork, and walk you through anything specific to that dog: medications, quirks, favorite toys. You'll have our cell numbers for questions big and small, and we check in every week to see how things are going and to field any adoption inquiries that come in.

08 How do I get started? +

Head to our Get Involved page and fill out the foster application. It takes about ten minutes. Or call us directly if you'd rather talk it through first, we're always glad to answer questions before anyone commits to anything.

Still deciding? Come meet a dog first.

There's no obligation in filling out an application, and no pressure once you do. Most foster parents tell us the hardest part was just making the call. We'd love to help you make it.
